# "At the time" notes: who held the ground, from the Record Atlas's context contract

Written 18 October 2026; amended 19 October 2026 (no snapshot change): a seeded era's note says "per the Record Atlas, drawn from this archive's own research" and carries `data-era-seeded="1"`, and the gate holds it to both (rule f); fifth version the same day (snapshot version 5, contract 1.11.0 and later for the rulers): the gate now re-derives every note from the snapshot, its place and its year (a mark taken off a built note fails as surely as one added), and an archive may opt in to naming the ruler (rule m). Fourth version the same day (snapshot version 4, contract 1.10.0 and later, 1.9.0 with a provisional fallback): an era the Atlas marks approximate, or contested about its bounds, is no longer thrown away whole but used for the years well inside it (rule l, the boundary margin). Second version the same day, rebuilt on the Atlas's versioned `context.json` (contract 1.7.0) in place of `regions.json`; third version the same day (snapshot version 3, contract 1.8.0 and later): the rings come from the contract, the style appears only on a page that has a note, a place near a ring edge keeps its note where the region across the edge has the same regime, and a note records whether the era it rests on was assessed. Pilot: the Mazza and Falco archives' person pages. Study behind it: the flags coverage study of the same day.

## What it is

Beside a dated place-event on a **deceased** person's page, a small muted line:

> Briatico · 1860 · *· At the time: Naples / the Two Sicilies* per the Record Atlas

It is **context about the ground, never a claim about the family**, it says where it came from, and where the data does not support it there is **nothing**: silence beats a wrong note. Text only: no flag image, no language of administration, no "before/after" events, no live fetch; the ruler is off unless the archive asks for it (rule m).

## The contract, and what this reads of it

`https://recordatlas.org/context.json` is documented at `/data/#context`. Each region era has a **permanent id** (`<region>--era-<from>`; the note's `data-era` is that id), `from`, `to` (`null` = still running, with `present: true`), `regimeLabel` (the display label), `polity`, `basis` (where the region's bands came from), `contested` and `approx`, and a `url` that lands on the era. The file also has a `rule` (how to look a year up), a `quality` block (gaps and overlaps, where a lookup must stay silent) and `countries` (the flag-page layer). Its `contract.version` is semantic: `erasnap.py` reads **major version 1** and refuses anything else, and records the version, the `generated` stamp and the sha256 of the file in the snapshot. From **1.8.0** each region also carries its `ring` and `bbox`, and the regions the Atlas has assessed carry `contested` and `approx` values, with a `why` where one is true. From **1.10.0** every assessed era also carries `fromMargin` and `toMargin` (whole years of uncertainty on its start and end year; 0 = the sources agree on one year; null = unknowable), `contestedScope` (`none` | `bounds` = the dispute is about when the change happened | `regime` = who held the ground in the middle was disputed | `part` = only some of the ring), `marginWhy` and `marginSourced` (false where the margin rests on dates the assessor knew but did not find in a fetched page). `approx` describes the BOUNDARY years only.

**Read:** `regions` only. **Not read:** `countries`. That layer is keyed on flag pages, most eras are unreviewed, and a sub-national place needs the sub-national answer; the archive's own place-to-region table decides which region a place is in, and a place no region covers gets no note (the contract's advice to "fall back to countries" is deliberately not taken).

**The rings are in the contract (from 1.8.0)**: each region's `ring` (`[lat, lon]` vertices, approximate by design) and `bbox` (`[south, west, north, east]`). `erasnap.py --contract` reads them by default and records `atlas.rings_source = "contract"`; it stops if a bbox is not its ring's own extent (the contract would contradict itself), and refuses a contract older than 1.8.0 unless the rings are given. `--rings <file>` (the Atlas's `regions.json`) is an **optional override**: the snapshot then says `rings_source = "file"` with the file's sha256, the file must describe the same regions and eras as the contract, and only the file carries the `regimes.existed` dates behind the "era outside the dates the Atlas holds for its regime" refusal (nothing the contract holds today trips it). `--no-rings` says no place is edge-checked.

## The rules, and why each exists

- **a. Region eras only**, mapped from the place by the archive's table (above).
- **b. The interval is half-open: `from <= year < to`**, as the contract states it. A note needs a place that maps to a region and a year inside exactly one era with a whole-number `from`, a `to` that is a whole number or `null`, and a non-empty **display label**. The label is the contract's `regimeLabel`, never the era's free-text `polity` (which can carry today's province: "Italy - Provincia di Vibo Valentia" for a town that is in Reggio). Two exceptions, both in `erasnap.py`: a regime the Atlas itself calls shorthand rather than a state (`feudal` = "Aquileia · Gorizia · Frankopan", `german` = "German occupation", `--refuse-regimes`) is refused; a regime that is a bare shorthand (`napo` = "Napoleonic", `--qualify-regimes`) is shown with the era's own polity ("Napoleonic — Illyrian Provinces"; "Napoleonic Kingdom of Naples" already begins with the label), and refused where the era has no polity. Nothing is invented. An era outside the dates the ring file holds for its regime (`existed`) is refused.
- **c. Never on a living, held-out or noindex page.** The library cannot know who is living, so the *page* must not call it for them, and `checkera.py` fails the build on a note found on a noindex page, a `data-living` page, or a page in the archive's living list.
- **d. A year on a boundary.** The contract's rule says a year equal to a boundary belongs to the newer era, and `erasnap.py` records `policy.boundary = "newer"` only if the contract's `rule` text says so (otherwise `"silent"`, and a boundary year gets no note; `--boundary silent` makes it stricter, never looser). The contract's own documentation adds that "a consumer holding only a year should say «in that year» or stay silent", and a year-only date cannot say which side of the change the event fell. So a boundary-year note resolves to the newer era (the contract's rule) but is **worded so that it is true either way**: `· From that year: <the newer label> per the Record Atlas`, marked `data-era-boundary="1"`, and checked by the gate. A year is a boundary only where another era (kept or refused) of the same region ends in that year; the first year of a region's first era, or of an era after a gap, is an ordinary year. A date the page marks uncertain ("c. 1860", "before 1860", "1860?") has no year at all: `plainYear(date, year)` gives a year only for "1860", "March 1860", "12 March 1860" or ISO, and only if it agrees with the number the archive holds. (Not handled, said plainly: events before 1752 in England, or in the Julian calendar, where the year begins on a different day. An archive that has them must hand `null`.)
- **e. The link goes to the era**, the contract's own `url` on the Atlas's base (the contract asks for a citation with a link to the era's url), accepted only if it is the path the id implies. `data-era` is the contract's era id.
- **f. Circularity.** An era whose `basis` begins `seeded:` (today `seeded:defranceschi-sovereignty`: istria-central, istria-south, istria-west, kvarner, carnia, dalmatia) was taken from this estate's own research: the Atlas's bands there are our own `sovereignty.json` handed back. "Per the Record Atlas" alone would then read as an independent second source confirming what the archive itself said, and it is not. The snapshot carries `seeded_from_archive` on the era (version 5 already does; the snapshot shape did not change for this), `eraAt` returns it as `seeded`, and the note says **"per the Record Atlas, drawn from this archive's own research"** (still a link to the era on the Atlas) and carries **`data-era-seeded="1"`**. It is never the plain phrase for a seeded era, and the mark is on no other note. `basis: "unknown"` (imotski: written in the same first batch, never checked) and `not-recorded` ("the file does not say") keep the standard words and no mark, and the basis is carried so an archive can count them. **An archive that wants its own words** passes them to the component, `<EraNote era={...} seededText="per the Atlas, which holds our own reading" />`, and to the gate, `checkera.py ... --seeded-text "per the Atlas, which holds our own reading"`: the gate then requires exactly those words on every seeded note (and the kit's on none). Whitespace is tidied; an empty text, or the plain phrase in any case or spacing, is not accepted (the component falls back to the kit's words; the gate refuses to run, exit 2). The gate recomputes `data-era-seeded` from the snapshot, like every other mark: a seeded era's note without the mark or the words fails (`seeded`, `source`, `text`), and so does a note on an era that is not seeded that carries the mark or the words.
- **g. null is not false; assessed is recorded.** The contract carries `contested` and `approx` as `null` on a region era when nobody has assessed it, `false` when someone looked and found it clean, and `true` (with a `why`) when it is a problem. An era is **assessed** when neither is null; the snapshot carries `assessed` and the contract's `why` on every kept era. `erasnap.py` **refuses** a null era by default (`--unassessed refuse`, reason "contested or approx not assessed (null)"); an era marked `true` is no longer refused whole but held to rule l (below). The library repeats the rule: an era whose flags are null is silent unless the snapshot's `policy.unassessed` is `"allow"`, and a flag that is neither true, false nor null is read as true. In contract 1.7.0 every one of the 347 region eras was null on both; from 1.8.0 some regions are assessed, so a build that refuses the unassessed keeps notes on those and no others. `--unassessed allow` is the deliberate, recorded choice to note the unassessed ones anyway: it is written into the snapshot's policy, and **each note says which kind it rests on** (`data-era-assessed="1"` on an assessed era, `"0"` on one the Atlas has not assessed; the library's `eraAt` returns `assessed` and `unassessed`). The gate prints, on every build, the counts of notes on assessed and on unassessed eras, fails a note whose mark disagrees with the snapshot, and `checkera.py --strict-unassessed` refuses the allow policy. The right fix is for the Atlas to assess eras; until it does, an archive that allows them is stating, in its own commit, that it accepts the Atlas's dates unassessed.
- **h. The quiet rule.** An event in the era that is still running (`to` null) in or after `recent_from` (default **1990**) gets no note: a modern event teaches nothing. Parameterised: `erasnap.py --recent-from N` writes it into the snapshot's policy and the gate holds the page to it; `eraAt(snap, place, year, { recentFrom: N })` overrides it for one call (`Infinity` switches it off); `eralib.era_at(..., recent_from=N)` the same in Python. It applies only to the running era: a closed era is not "modern".
- **i. Edge: quiet only when it matters.** The rings are 4-11 vertex shapes, "approximate by design", so a town near a ring edge may belong to the region across it. With the place's coordinates, `erasnap.py` finds a place that is within `--edge-km` (default **15**) of its own region's ring edge, inside or outside it (**near an edge**), or inside another region's ring and not its own (**in conflict**), and for it the **neighbours**: the other regions whose ring contains the place or lies within `--edge-km` of it. A place the archive's table marks `"edge": true` is silent (`marked-edge`). The distance is measured in a flat projection around the point (1 degree of latitude is 111.195 km, longitude scaled by cos latitude) to the ring's edges as straight lines between its `[lat, lon]` vertices.
  - **Where nothing across the edge can claim it, or the neighbours cannot be known**, the place is refused for every year, as before: no neighbour to compare (the place may be on ground no region covers), or some region of the contract has no usable ring. It is listed in `places_silent` ("edge", or "ring-conflict" for a place inside another's ring) so it can be counted.
  - **Where neighbours exist**, the place is kept with `edge_neighbours: {kind: "edge"|"ring-conflict", regions: [...]}` and rule **j** decides each year. The Strait of Messina is the case: Calabria and Sicily are both the Two Sicilies 1816-1861 and Italy from 1861, so a note at Scilla, Bagnara, Reggio or Messina is true on whichever side of the ring the town stands, in those years; in a year the two differ there is no note.
  - `--edge strict` is the old rule (every near-edge place refused); the snapshot's `policy.edge` says which was used.
  - A place far outside its own ring and near no other is kept and counted (`--outside-ring refuse` silences it). A place with **no coordinates cannot be checked**: it is kept, listed in `unchecked_edge`, and counted (`eraAt(...).unchecked` is true), so an archive can say how many of its notes rest on a table entry alone.
- **j. Edge agreement (the library, per year).** For a place in `edge_neighbours`, the note needs, in **every** neighbouring region, in that year: exactly one kept era that covers it; no other era of that region overlapping it (anywhere); no refused era over the year; neither `approx` nor `contested` true, nor null unless the policy allows it; and the **same label** as the place's own era (the display label, compared as written). Anything else is unknown, and unknown is silence: `whyNot` says `edge` (or `ring-conflict`). If the year is not a boundary of the place's own era but is the first year of a neighbour's era, the neighbour's era just before it must exist, be kept and carry the same label (the event may be on either side of that change); a boundary note ("From that year: X") needs only the neighbour's era in force. Where all agree, the note is drawn and says so: `eraAt(...).edgeAgree` is true and the note carries `data-era-edge-agree="1"`. A snapshot entry the library cannot read (no kind, an unknown kind, no regions, the place's own region as a neighbour) is silence.
- **k. The style belongs to a page that has a note.** The component used to carry a scoped style, which Astro bundles into every page that renders the component whether or not it draws a note (+470 KB raw, +193 KB gzip across one 4,000-page archive; +126 KB across another). The note now emits one `<style is:inline data-era-css>` (the rules are `ERA_CSS` in `lib/era.js`: muted, token-driven, as before) from the **first note a page renders**, once per page (`claim(Astro.request, ERA_CSS_KEY)`, the mechanism `Rec.astro` uses for its figure ids), and a page that draws no note carries no era CSS. The `<style>` sits in the flow ahead of that note; a text extractor that reads built HTML must skip `<style>` as it skips `<script>` (the kit's own tools do). The gate fails a page with a note and no style, a style drawn twice, or a style on a page with no note (`css`).

- **l. The boundary margin: approximate and contested eras, used for the years well inside them.** The Atlas's uncertainty is about the BOUNDS of an era (Casalanza or Vienna 1815; the Two Sicilies formally 1816; Garibaldi 1860 or annexation 1861; Queensland's separation 1859), not about what held in the middle: an event in 1830 in Calabria is under the Two Sicilies whichever of those years is right. So an era that is flagged `approx`, flagged `contested` about its bounds, or that carries margins, is used only for a year **more than the reach from each numeric bound** (the start; the end only if the era is closed):

  `year - from > max(N, fromMargin)`  and  `to - year > max(N, toMargin)`

  where **N** is the snapshot's `policy.margin_years` (default **5**; `0` switches the floor off) and the margins are the contract's own `fromMargin` / `toMargin` (1.10.0 and later). The reach is **more than** the margin, not "at least": a boundary uncertain by 2 years may lie 2 years either side, so the first safe year is 3 away. A margin of 0 on a bound the floor does not lift (N = 0) is a clean date: a boundary year then takes the newer era as rule d says.
  - **Unsourced margins are softer.** Where the contract's `marginSourced` is false (the assessor knew the date but did not find it in a fetched page) the reach is `max(N, margin) + N`. The snapshot carries `margin_sourced`; a note resting on such a margin carries `data-era-margin-unsourced="1"`, and the gate counts these separately. PROVISIONAL: the Atlas has not said how much softer.
  - **A null margin is unknowable**: the era is refused by the builder ("a bound whose margin is unknowable"), so is a margin that is not a whole number of years. The end of an era still running is no bound. An unassessed era (flags null) has null margins too, so it stays refused even under `--unassessed allow`.
  - **`contestedScope`.** `none` and `bounds` are used; `regime` and `part` are silent whatever the margins (a part cannot be told from the ring); **a contested era with no scope is silent**. `--contested scope` (the default) is exactly that; `--contested margin` is the what-if for a contract before 1.10.0 that has no scope (a contested era with no scope is used, held by the floor alone: a recorded choice, not a finding; regime and part stay refused); `--contested refuse` refuses every contested era. `--approx margin` (default) uses approx eras by the rule; `--approx refuse` refuses them.
  - **Before 1.10.0 (no margin fields at all)** an approx era gets margin 0 on each side, recorded as `margin_src: "fallback"`: only the floor N holds it. This is **PROVISIONAL and not an Atlas fact**; the gate prints how many notes rest on it. A contested-about-bounds era with no margin from the Atlas is refused ("no margin to hold the year to"): the fallback is for an approx era only.
  - **The archive may only widen**: `erasnap.py --margins <file>` ({era id: years | {from, to}}) raises the margin of single eras; it can never lower the Atlas's. The snapshot records them in `policy.margins` and `margin_src: "archive"`.
  - **The shorthand regimes stay refused** (`feudal`, `german`, rule b) under every policy: the Atlas's bare 1200 "feudal" eras are "we do not know", not a state.
  - **The note's words claim no more than the Atlas does**: the regime label and "per the Record Atlas", no bound of the era in them. A note on an approx era carries `data-era-approx="1"`, on a contested one `data-era-contested="1"`, on an unsourced margin `data-era-margin-unsourced="1"` (like `data-era-assessed`), and the gate checks each against the snapshot.
  - **The decision is recorded and re-derived.** Each kept era with a margin or a flag carries `margin_ok {kind, first, last}`: the first and last year a note may use (`last` null: still running). The library recomputes it from the margins and N and treats a record that does not agree as silence (`whyNot` says `bad-margin`); the gate fails the snapshot as malformed. A year inside the reach is `margin`.
  - Policy table:

    | policy | values (default first) | effect |
    |---|---|---|
    | `approx` | `margin`, `refuse` | an approx era is used by the rule, or refused |
    | `contested` | `scope`, `margin`, `refuse` | contested: only scope none/bounds; or also no-scope (what-if); or never |
    | `margin_years` | 5 | N, the floor of every reach (PROVISIONAL: not an Atlas figure) |
    | `margins` | `{}` | per-era widening by the archive |
    | `unassessed` | `refuse`, `allow` | as rule g |

  Counts on a real archive (Mazza, 18 Oct 2026, same 54 places): contract 1.8.0, 210 notes; 1.9.0 strict (no approx, no contested), 27; 1.9.0 approx by margin 5 and contested refused (no scope in 1.9.0), 169; 1.9.0 with `--contested margin`, 182; **1.10.0 default, 167** (Calabria and Sicily are mostly silent only within 5 years of 1815/1816/1861; the Sicily 1816 era is `regime`-contested, which silences the Scilla and Bagnara notes that depend on it).

- **m. Rulers: who reigned, only where it is certain, and only if the archive asks.** From contract 1.11.0 a region may carry `rulers` (today `calabria` only: 64 reigns) and `language`; `hasRulers` and `hasLanguage` are false elsewhere, and a contract before 1.11.0 has neither field. **A missing field is not "none":** it is unknown, and unknown is silence. A reign row is `name`, `polity` (free text), `polityId` (a stable slug: `kingdom-of-sicily`, `kingdom-of-naples`, `parthenopean-republic`, `kingdom-of-the-two-sicilies`, `kingdom-of-italy`, `italian-republic`), `from` / `to` as the source states them, numeric `fromYear` / `toYear` (`toYear` null: still reigning), `fromMargin` / `toMargin` (0 where a page gives a day for that end, 1 where only a year or a season, and 1 for a legal boundary known to differ in fact: the end of Francis II and the start of Victor Emmanuel II in autumn 1860 / 17 March 1861), `marginWhy`, `q` (a Wikidata id or null), `source` and `note`. A row is the legal reign a page gives, not who held the ground in fact. **Language is not used.**

  - **What is shown** (and only when the archive passes `rulers`): `· At the time: Naples / the Two Sicilies (Ferdinand II) per the Record Atlas`. The name is the Atlas's `name` exactly (not translated, not normalised: "Philip II (listed as Philip III of Spain)" is shown as that), the polity shown is the regime label (never the `polity` string), and "per the Record Atlas" stays, always. The note carries `data-era-ruler="<name>"` and a `span.era-ruler`. Off by default: nothing changes anywhere until an archive opts in.
  - **What is refused (the ruler is left out, the note stays), and why.** `rulerAt(snapshot, region, year)` names a ruler only when **all** hold; `whyNoRuler` says which fails first:
    1. `snapshot` / `year` / `no-region`: the snapshot is this version, the year is a whole number, the region is held.
    2. `no-rulers`: the snapshot says `rulers[region].has === true` (the contract's `hasRulers`). False, null (the contract did not say) and an absent entry are all silence.
    3. `bad-rows`: every reign row is usable (a name, a polityId slug, whole-number years, whole-number margins). One unusable row silences the region: it might be the one that overlaps the year.
    4. `no-era`: the region has exactly one kept era over the year and none refused. The ruler is context to the era's note, never alone.
    5. `no-reign` / `overlap`: **exactly one** reign touches the year, where a reign touches `[fromYear - fromMargin, toYear + toMargin]` (an open reign has no end). Two reigns that overlap there are null, never a pick: Joanna III and Charles IV (1516-1554), Frederick II and Constance (1198), the changeover year of every reign.
    6. `polity`: the reign's `polityId` is one `policy.ruler_polities` allows for the era's **regime**. A Naples reign under a Two Sicilies era is null; so is any regime the table does not list. The kit's default table is `twosicilies` -> `kingdom-of-the-two-sicilies` and `italy` -> `kingdom-of-italy`, `italian-republic` (an era labelled "Italy" runs from the Kingdom into the Republic). The Napoleonic and shorthand regimes are not listed, so they name no ruler. `erasnap.py --ruler-polities <file>` replaces the table, on the record (it is in the snapshot's policy).
    7. `margin`: the same discipline as rule l. `year - fromYear > max(N, fromMargin)` and, for a closed reign, `toYear - year > max(N, toMargin)`, with N = `policy.margin_years` (default 5, the same floor). The reach is **more than** the margin: the first safe year after a reign starts is `fromYear + max(N, fromMargin) + 1`. A reign of day-precision dates is therefore named only in its middle: Calabria's Ferdinand II (1830-1859) from 1836 to 1853.
  - **The note-level rules.** A boundary-year note ("From that year") names no ruler (it claims the newer ground, not who reigned); an edge note (a place held to its neighbours, rule j) names none (a neighbour's reign was not compared). The note itself must exist first: the era rules (a-l) run before the ruler is looked at, so a year within the era's margin or the quiet rule has no note and so no ruler.
  - **The snapshot carries it.** `erasnap.py` copies a region's `rulers` into `rulers[region]` = `{has, why, reigns}` for the regions the snapshot holds, with `has` true only where the contract says `hasRulers: true` **and** every row is usable and its years follow the dates it states (a closed reign with no `toYear`, which would read as one that goes on for ever, refuses the region). The row's `note` and the whole `language` block are not carried. Because the shape changed, **the snapshot is version 5**; versions 1 to 4 are refused, consistently, and an archive that takes this kit rebuilds its snapshot in the same commit as the pin.
  - **The gate checks it.** `checkera.py` re-derives the ruler for the note's place and year and fails (category `ruler`) a ruler whose reign does not cover the year, overlaps another reign, lies within the margin, is of a polity the regime does not allow, is on a boundary or edge note, is on a region with no rulers, or whose name is not the Atlas's exactly (the attribute and the words must both be it). With `--rulers` (the archive's statement that it has opted in) it also fails a note whose snapshot names a ruler and that does not carry it. Without `--rulers` a ruler that is present is still verified.

- **n. The gate re-derives the note.** Until the fifth version the gate checked each mark against the era the note named, so a mark taken off a built note (`data-era-edge-agree` above all, which has no era-level fact behind it) passed. Every note now carries `data-era-place`, the folded place it was drawn for, and the gate calls the library's own `era_at(snapshot, place, year)` for it and holds the note to the answer: a place that does not map (`place`), a note the library would not have drawn at all (`silent`), the era (`place`), every mark **missing, extra or wrong** (`boundary`, `assessed`, `approx` for approx / contested / margin-unsourced, `edge`), the ruler (`ruler`), and the note's whole text, which must be exactly the mark, the label, the ruler if any, and the source (`text`; `label` and `source` as before). A planted fault for each mark, removed and added, is in `era_selftest.py` and `era_logic.mjs`.

## The snapshot (`era-snapshot.json`, committed by the archive; version 5)

The sites are static and the Atlas's files change daily, so a note cannot fetch. The archive commits a snapshot, refreshes it deliberately with `erasnap.py`, and reviews the diff. Nothing in a build reads the Atlas.

```
version 5, built
contract   { name, version, generated, sha256 }          the contract it was cut from (sha256 of the file's bytes)
atlas      { base, rings_source: contract|file|none, rings_sha256 (the override file's, else null) }
policy     { unassessed: refuse|allow, boundary: newer|silent, recent_from: 1990, edge_km: 15, edge: agree|strict,
             approx: margin|refuse, contested: scope|margin|refuse, margin_years: 5, margins: {era id: {from, to}},
             ruler_polities: {regime id: [polityId]} }
regions    { region id: [ {id, from, to|null, present, regime, label, polity, basis, seeded_from_archive, approx, contested,
                           assessed, why|null, url, from_margin, to_margin, margin_src: contract|archive|fallback|null,
                           margin_sourced: true|false|null, contested_scope: none|bounds|regime|part|null, margin_ok: {kind, first, last}|null} ] }   (the regions the places use, and the neighbours of the edge places)
places     { folded place: region id }                     fold() of the place as the archive prints it
places_silent  { folded place: edge|marked-edge|ring-conflict }
edge_neighbours { folded place: {kind: edge|ring-conflict, regions: [region id]} }   mapped near an edge: the regions it is held to
unchecked_edge [ folded place ]                            mapped, but no coordinates: the edge rule could not be applied
dropped    [ {region, id, from, to, reason} ]              eras refused (so a count of what was left silent can say why)
quality    { counts: {gaps, overlaps}, gaps: [...], overlaps: [...] }   the contract's own lists, the entries that name a region here
rulers     { region id: {has: true|false|null, why: text|null, reigns: [ {name, polity_id, from, to, from_year, to_year, from_margin, to_margin,
                                                                           margin_why, q, source} ] } }   rule m: has is true only with usable rows
```

An era the contract's `quality` block lists in a gap or an overlap is refused ("listed in the contract's quality gaps/overlaps"; today the lists name flag pages only, never a region era). Two eras of one region that overlap are both kept (not flagged: the contract never said they were contested) and the library stays silent about every year they touch.

The archive's place table is `{place as printed: region id}` (or `{region, lat, lon, edge}`; `null` or `-` says "looked at, no region covers it").

```
python3 node_modules/@daviddef/archive-kit/kit/tools/erasnap.py \
   --contract "<Record Atlas>/site/public/context.json" \
   --places src/data/era-places.json --out src/data/era-snapshot.json [--unassessed allow] [--approx margin|refuse] [--contested scope|margin|refuse] [--margin-years N] [--margins file.json] [--ruler-polities file.json] [--report drops.json]
python3 .../erasnap.py ... --check      # in CI: the committed snapshot is what the inputs build
```

`--report` writes, per region, how many eras were refused and why (counts only): that is what to send the Atlas session. The old `regions.json`-as-source form of this builder is gone: the contract is the source. **The library and the gate read version 5 only**: a snapshot of version 1 to 4 is refused (a version 4 snapshot has no `rulers` and no `ruler_polities`, and a version 3 one has no margins, so it would say nothing of the years near an era's bounds, and the library must not read it as if it had), so an archive that takes this kit rebuilds its snapshot with it, in the same commit as the pin. What an archive picks up: bump the pin; rebuild the snapshot from a contract (1.10.0 or later for the Atlas's own margins, 1.11.0 or later for the rulers; the defaults are `--approx margin --contested scope --margin-years 5`); the gate prints notes on approximate and contested eras, on the provisional floor alone, and on unsourced margins; any tool that matches the note's attribute list must allow `data-era-approx`, `data-era-contested` and `data-era-margin-unsourced` after `data-era-assessed`, then `data-era-edge-agree`, then `data-era-place` (always) and `data-era-ruler` (only when rulers are on) last, and a `span.era-ruler` between the label and the source. Changes to the call: `--rings` is no longer needed (drop it, or keep it as the override), `--edge agree|strict` is new (default agree), and the snapshot carries `atlas.rings_source`, `policy.edge`, `edge_neighbours` and `assessed`/`why` on each era.

## Wiring an archive

```astro
---
import { eraAt, plainYear } from "@daviddef/archive-kit/lib/era.js";
import EraNote from "@daviddef/archive-kit/components/EraNote.astro";
import snapshot from "../../data/era-snapshot.json";
const noNotes = living || meta.noindex;                   // rule c, decided by the page
---
<div class="v">{e.place}{!noNotes && <EraNote era={eraAt(snapshot, e.place, plainYear(e.date, e.year))} />}</div>
```

(The call is the same since version 1; the component draws its own style, once, on a page that has a note, so the archive adds no CSS for it.) After the build, `python3 .../checkera.py --dist dist --snapshot src/data/era-snapshot.json --living-urls <the archive's living list> --require-tracked`.

**Markup tools.** Every archive has tools that match the note's markup (a living-page or prose scanner, a link checker, an allowed-attribute list): make them allow `data-era-seeded` (new: on the note of a seeded era, rule f, whether or not the archive uses rulers), `data-era-place` and, with rulers, `data-era-ruler` and `span.era-ruler`, as well as the marks they already allow (`data-era`, `data-era-year`, `data-era-boundary`, `data-era-assessed`, `data-era-approx`, `data-era-contested`, `data-era-margin-unsourced`, `data-era-edge-agree`). A seeded era's note reads "per the Record Atlas, drawn from this archive's own research" (or the archive's `seededText`), so a tool that matches the source words must accept that too.

**To opt in to rulers** (rule m; today only a place in Calabria can have one): rebuild the snapshot from a contract of 1.11.0 or later (`erasnap.py` carries the rulers and the default `ruler_polities`); pass `rulers` to the component, `<EraNote era={eraAt(snapshot, e.place, plainYear(e.date, e.year))} rulers />`; add `--rulers` to the `check:era` command; make any tool that matches the note's markup allow `data-era-place`, `data-era-ruler` and `span.era-ruler`. Nothing else changes, and an archive that does not do these things gets the notes it always had (plus `data-era-place`). The gate reads the **built** pages: a template that is right can still be given a wrong year. It checks that every `data-era` is an era id the snapshot holds, that `data-era-year` satisfies `from <= year < to`, that the quiet rule and the boundary wording hold, that the label is the snapshot's, that the source words and the era link are the right ones, that `data-era-assessed` says what the snapshot says of the era, that `data-era-edge-agree` is only on a region that has an edge place, that the era style is on every page with a note exactly once and on no page without one, that no note is on a noindex or living page, and that the snapshot is committed. Since the fifth version it also **re-derives each note from the snapshot, its place and its year** (rule n) and checks the ruler (rule m). It prints **counts only** (notes on assessed and on unassessed eras, at an edge where every neighbour agrees, on a boundary year, on a seeded region, naming a ruler; pages that carry the style). It refuses an empty build and a build with no notes (exit 2): a gate that found nothing has not found that nothing is wrong.

## Counting what was left silent

`whyNot(snapshot, place, year, opts)` returns one of `snapshot`, `year`, `place`, `marked-edge`, `no-region`, `no-era`, `era-refused`, `contested`, `recent`, `boundary`, `approx`, `margin`, `bad-margin`, `unassessed`, `no-label`, `bad-id`, `edge`, `ring-conflict`, or `ok`. (`edge` and `ring-conflict` are also what a place refused for every year by the builder gives, first; for an edge place kept with neighbours they come last: only when nothing else would have silenced the year.) An archive that reports "n events, k notes, the rest silent because ..." should call it for every event, with `plainYear` for the year (a date that is uncertain is `year`).

## What this does not do, on purpose

No flag images in the note (the ribbon cards' mini flags, which follow these rules and add their own, are `docs/ribbon-flags.md`; the art is used with David's say and no open licence is claimed), no ruler unless the archive asks and the Atlas states one for exactly that year (rule m), no language of administration (the contract's `language` block is not read), no "life in context" strip, no country-level fallback, no live fetch. The label is the Atlas's **regime** label ("Naples / the Two Sicilies", "Italy", "Britain and its colonies"), which is a filing regime named in the Atlas's own words, not always the state's formal name. That is why every note says whose words they are.
